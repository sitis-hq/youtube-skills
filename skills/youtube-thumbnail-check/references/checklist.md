# Judging a thumbnail

Rewritten from youtube-seo-skills (`youtube-seo-thumbnail`, commit 779b0f13) and claude-youtube
(`sub-skills/thumbnail.md`, `references/thumbnail-ctr-guide.md`, commit 84c3fa68), both MIT. Their
unsourced percentages (face +20–30 % CTR, niche CTR tables, CLIP thresholds) are left out, and their
A/B section is corrected: YouTube judges thumbnail tests by watch-time share, not by CTR.

## Specification

From YouTube Help "Add video thumbnails" (support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431), read 2026-11-04:

| | Videos | Shorts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aspect | 16:9 | 9:16 |
| Recommended | 3840×2160 | 2160×3840 |
| Minimum | 640 px wide | 640 px wide and tall |
| Format | JPG or PNG | JPG or PNG |
| File size | 2 MB from the mobile app; 50 MB from desktop and `thumbnails.set` | same |
| Who can | a verified account (phone verification) | the same |

Older guides say "1280×720, under 2 MB": that was the previous recommendation and still uploads, but
export the master at 3840×2160 and a JPG/PNG copy that fits the route it goes up by.

## Judging

Work at feed size first. A thumbnail is seen at roughly 120–250 px wide in suggestions and phone
feeds; the script's previews are those sizes.

1. **Legibility at 120–168 px — the gate.** Can you tell what the subject is, read the text, and
   read the emotion? Any "no" is critical. Detail that disappears at this size is noise at full size.
2. **One focal point.** One subject the eye lands on first; everything else supports it. Two
   competing subjects read as clutter when small.
3. **Text: short, not the title.** At most three or four words, ideally one to three. The title
   carries the keyword and the promise; the thumbnail adds what the title does not say (a result,
   a stake, a reaction). Any word repeated from the title is a wasted surface.
4. **Text is readable.** Heavy sans-serif, a light/dark contrast with what is behind it (stroke,
   shadow or a plain panel), not crossing the face or the focal point.
5. **Bottom-right is taken** by the duration stamp, and the top-right by the menu and "watch later"
   controls on hover: nothing essential there. The previews draw the stamp.
6. **Contrast and colour.** The subject separates from the background (light, colour, blur). Two or
   three dominant colours; one accent. It must stand out on both the white and the near-black YouTube
   themes. Compare `contrast_stddev` between variants of the same design; there is no absolute bar.
7. **Face, when the format has one.** Large enough to read the expression at 168 px; an authentic,
   readable emotion matching the video's tone; a neutral face adds little. Gaze can lead to the
   subject or the text. Not every niche needs a face: music releases, products and places work
   with the object itself.
8. **Different from the neighbours.** Against the competitor thumbnails of the same search or
   suggestions, it should not use the same palette and layout. Use `--compare-dir` to find the most
   similar ones, then look at them.
9. **Honest.** What the thumbnail shows happens in the video. A promise the video does not keep loses
   watch time, which is also what A/B tests measure, and misleading thumbnails can be removed under
   YouTube's policies.
10. **Consistent with the channel.** Same typeface family and accent colours as the channel's other
    thumbnails and its banner, so a subscriber recognises it in the feed.
11. **Shorts.** The Shorts feed mostly shows the video itself; a chosen frame or uploaded cover
    matters on the channel page and in search. Keep the subject centred: the shelf crops.

Report issues as Critical (fails 1, 3 or 9), High (2, 4, 5), Medium (6, 7, 8), Low (10, 11), each
with the preview or measurement that shows it.

## Redesign brief

Specific enough that an image model or a designer needs no follow-up. Images are generated by the
`poyo` tool attached to the step: write the prompt as plain natural language describing the scene,
not as tool flags. Render text on the image only if the model renders it cleanly; otherwise leave
clean space and add the text afterwards.

```
Title it goes with: <title>   (the thumbnail adds: <what the title does not say>)
Focal subject: <who/what>, <position, e.g. right third>, about <share> of the frame
Expression (if a face): <precise emotion>, gaze <to camera / to the text / to the object>
Text: "<1-3 words>", <heavy sans-serif>, <colour> on <background> with <stroke/shadow>, <position away from bottom-right>
Background: <setting>, <blur/plain/gradient>, separated from the subject by <light/colour>
Palette: <2-3 hex colours + accent>, checked against both YouTube themes
Different from competitors: <the pattern they share and how this breaks it>
Do not include: <at least three concrete things, e.g. a fourth word, small logos, detail in the corners>
Export: 3840x2160 master; JPG/PNG copy under the upload route's size limit
```

## Test & Compare

From YouTube Help "Test & compare thumbnails and titles" (support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714),
read 2026-11-04:

- Up to three titles and/or thumbnails per test, set up in YouTube Studio on a computer only. There is
  no API: the step runs it through the browser (`youtube-studio`) under an intent reservation.
- **The winner is the variant with the highest watch time** (watch-time share), not the highest CTR;
  YouTube may keep a small control group on the original. A variant with fewer clicks but longer
  viewing can win.
- Results: Winner, Performed same, Inconclusive. A test should finish within two weeks; it can take
  a few days.
- Needs advanced features enabled. Not available for Shorts, scheduled lives, Premieres before they
  end, made-for-kids, mature-audience or private videos.
- Changing the title or thumbnail during the test stops it.

Designing the variants:

- **Change exactly one variable per variant** (the text, the expression, the colour, the
  composition) against the current one, so a result says which change mattered.
- State each variant's hypothesis in one sentence and which viewers it is for (search vs browse).
- Run every variant through `analyze_thumbnail.py`; all must pass the 120–168 px gate before testing.
- Read the outcome as YouTube reports it. "Performed same" or "Inconclusive" is a result: keep the
  original and test a bigger change next time, do not re-run the same pair.
